Ladder Precedent Audit

Every architectural and training decision in the Perception Ladder Spec, checked against the methodology sections of the primary papers — not their abstracts — and marked Direct, Partial, or Novel.

Verified in session 9 papers, full text extracted Marked for checking 8 claims Nothing implemented
Headline — read this before the tables

The literature does not reject this direction, but it contradicts the specification on two points that matter more than everything else combined.

1 · Every strong CARLA RL result runs PPO on privileged state and trains the encoder end-to-end. Roach (ICCV 2021) trains PPO on a rendered privileged BEV, then distils to a camera student by imitation. CaRL (CoRL 2025) does the same at 300 M samples on 8×A100 and explicitly states the encoder "is trained with PPO". Nobody in the CARLA literature runs PPO downstream of a frozen learned sensor encoder as the primary driving policy. That paradigm exists — but in robotic manipulation and navigation, not driving.

2 · The sample budget is off by more than two orders of magnitude. The spec proposes 1.5 M steps. Roach needs 10 M for a privileged-BEV policy. CaRL needs 300 M for 64 DS. Even granting that a 2-d residual around a competent nominal trajectory is a far easier RL problem, 1.5 M is not a defensible number and would very likely produce a flat ladder that says nothing about perception.

2 · Contradictions between the papers

These are real disagreements in the literature, not variations in wording. The specification must take a side on each and say which.

QuestionPosition APosition BWhat actually differs, and our stance
Where does RL sit? Upstream, on privileged state. Roach and CaRL both train PPO on a rendered BEV with full ground truth; the sensor agent is then trained by imitation. Downstream, on frozen features. Parisi et al. detach a frozen pre-trained encoder and train the policy on top of it. Not a wording difference — opposite orderings. A is validated in driving; B is validated in manipulation and navigation. We are doing B in a domain where only A has been shown. That is our biggest exposure and also, framed properly, our clearest novelty.
Multi-view: fuse tokens or stitch pixels? Stitch. TransFuser crops three 120° cameras into one 704×160, 132° image and runs a single backbone. Tokenise per view. InterFuser and LMDrive keep views separate and concatenate tokens. A is cheaper and TransFuser's ablation shows FOV dominates; B scales to non-overlapping views (rear) and lets attention weight views differently. We follow B, and must beat A's FOV effect to show it matters.
RGB+LiDAR: where to fuse? Multi-resolution. TransFuser fuses at several feature-map scales with self-attention plus velocity embeddings. Once, late. InterFuser fuses in a single transformer stage over pooled tokens. A is the stronger-validated design for exactly the RGB+LiDAR pairing we are testing; B is what our spec proposes. A genuine risk that our R3 under-reads LiDAR's value because of the fusion stage, not the sensor.
Output representation Waypoints. TransFuser, LBC, InterFuser, LMDrive. Direct control. Roach and CaRL both output steering + acceleration; Roach argues explicitly that "a PID-controller is not reliable for trajectory tracking and requires excessive parameter tuning." The split tracks the learner: IL papers predict waypoints, RL papers predict controls. CarLLaVA sits between — waypoints are poor in turns, direct control is poor at collision avoidance — and proposes a semi-disentangled path + waypoint output. Our residual-on-trajectory design is a fourth position and is not directly precedented.
Is pooling harmful? No. TransFuser average-pools to 512-d then compresses to a 64-d vector and was SOTA. Use queries. InterFuser and LMDrive use learned queries instead. The earlier audit called mean-pooling "the biggest architectural flaw"; that was too strong. TransFuser shows a tiny pooled vector suffices for waypoint decoding. Queries are better motivated for referring expressions, which is a language argument, not a perception one.
Does language need the nav command removed? Remove it. LangAuto gives no discrete command and no target waypoint — language is the only navigation signal. Keep both. Most CARLA benchmarks supply a discrete command; language would then be supplementary. Nobody in the verified set keeps both and shows language still helps for navigation. Where language demonstrably adds value alongside a nav channel is the notice case. That should be the centre of our language rung, not the junction case.

3 · Rung-by-rung audit

R0Blind — ego + nav only
Validated by
Roach's ablation is the closest analogue: baseline PPO with a Gaussian policy "is trapped in a local minimum where staying still is the most rewarding strategy." A degenerate-behaviour control is standard practice, though usually reported as a failure mode rather than a designed rung.
Established
That reward functions in CARLA admit trivial stationary solutions, and that this must be detected explicitly.
Our adaptation
Promoting it from a footnote to a first-class rung and a hard gate.
Novel
Using a blind rung as a leakage detector with a numeric threshold. No direct precedent found. Defensible and cheap.
Hidden confound
Real. A target point at 25–40 m plus a 6-way command may be sufficient to lane-follow Town01, whose topology is simple and largely orthogonal. If R0 scores well the correct response is to push the target point further out — not to conclude the environment is broken. Decide the threshold before seeing the number.
R1Front RGB
Validated by
Learning by Cheating (single forward camera → waypoints), CILRS, CarLLaVA (camera-only, 1st in CARLA Challenge 2.0).
Established
A single forward camera plus a nav command is sufficient for competitive closed-loop CARLA driving.
Our adaptation
Query-based encoder rather than a ResNet+branch head; PPO residual rather than pure IL.
Novel
Nothing. This rung is well-trodden, which is exactly what a baseline should be.
Hidden confound
Severe, and it propagates to R2. TransFuser's ablation finds "Camera FOV has the largest impact on the DS", and they reach 132° with three stitched cameras. If R1's front camera uses the legacy 125° FOV, it already covers most of what R2's side cameras add, and R2 will measure near-nothing. R1's FOV must be pre-registered — a realistic single production forward camera, ~60–70°, so that R2 has headroom. Otherwise R2 is not a test of multi-view, it is a test of stitching.
R2Four RGB views, per-view tokens
Validated by
InterFuser (left/front/right/focus + LiDAR, transformer decoder) and LMDrive (multi-view tokens concatenated before the BEV decoder). Not TransFuser, which stitches.
Established
Per-view tokenisation with a shared fusion stage is a working design in CARLA closed-loop driving.
Our adaptation
A rear view, which neither InterFuser nor TransFuser uses; LMDrive does. And learned view embeddings as the view identifier.
Novel
Isolating multi-view benefit as a controlled ablation at matched parameters. The papers use multi-view; none ablates it against a single view with parameters held constant.
Hidden confound
Two. The FOV issue inherited from R1. And the rear camera contributes almost nothing to forward driving in the CARLA route benchmarks — it matters for lane changes, which our route set may rarely require. Pre-register the sub-metric (junction and side-impact infractions) and check that the route set actually contains lane-change situations.
R3+ LiDAR BEV
Validated by
TransFuser — the strongest evidence in the set, with explicit sensor ablations, and a stated 48% reduction in collisions per km versus geometry-based fusion. InterFuser corroborates.
Established
Attention-based RGB+LiDAR fusion reduces collisions in dense CARLA traffic. The 2-bin height-histogram BEV is the validated representation, and it is better than our single max-height channel — adopt theirs.
Our adaptation
Single late fusion stage instead of multi-resolution fusion; no velocity embedding.
Novel
Nothing architecturally. The ablation-at-matched-parameters framing is ours.
Hidden confound
Two, one of them serious. (1) TransFuser rasterises the goal location into the LiDAR BEV as a third channel — so their "LiDAR helps" result partly measures a better goal encoding. If we copy their input format we inherit the confound; our spec must keep the goal out of the BEV, and we should say why. (2) Fusing once, late, may under-read LiDAR's contribution relative to TransFuser's multi-resolution fusion — so a null R3 result would be ambiguous between "LiDAR doesn't help" and "our fusion stage is too shallow". Mitigate by running the multi-resolution variant in the R5 family.
R4+ language
Validated by
LMDrive and the LangAuto benchmark, including the Notice track — thorough precedent for every element: instruction taxonomy, LLM paraphrasing, closed-loop evaluation, and the DS/RC/IS metric set.
Established
Language-conditioned closed-loop CARLA driving works; notice instructions are a recognised category with a dedicated evaluation track; instruction paraphrasing via LLMs is standard.
Our adaptation
A frozen sentence encoder with cached embeddings instead of a 7 B LLM; partial rather than total suppression of the nav command; cross-attention conditioning rather than an LLM forward pass.
Novel
Language conditioning without a language model in the loop, at ~25 M parameters instead of ~7 B, evaluated with a counterfactual triad. The counterfactual triad specifically (correct / shuffled / null on one trained policy) — no direct precedent found in the verified set; LMDrive tests misleading instructions, which is adjacent but not the same control.
Hidden confound
The redundancy problem, and it is the one that decides this rung. LMDrive removes the discrete command entirely; we keep it except at designated junctions. Any residual redundancy lets the policy ignore language and still complete routes. The notice-instruction family (IC-5) is the part of R4 least exposed to this, because no nav channel can carry it — make that the primary R4 claim, not the junction case.

4 · The eight specific challenges

AIs a fixed 128-d latent a scientifically defensible control?Novel
Yes, and it is one of the stronger parts of the design — but it is our argument, not a citation.

TransFuser passes a 64-d vector to its GRU across configurations, so fixed-width bottlenecks are ordinary practice. What no paper in the verified set does is hold latent width and parameter count constant specifically so that a sensor ablation is uncontaminated by capacity. Sensor ablations in TransFuser vary the sensor and let the representation follow.

Two caveats worth writing into the methods section. First, a fixed width is not a fixed information capacity — a richer sensor set may saturate 128 dimensions where a poorer one does not, so at some point the bottleneck itself becomes the treatment. Mitigate by running one width sweep (64 / 128 / 256) on the richest rung and showing the result is flat in that range. Second, matched parameters plus matched width still leaves matched FLOPs unmatched; report FLOPs alongside so the reader can see it. NOVEL PROPOSAL — NO DIRECT PRECEDENT FOUND. Defensible, and worth arguing explicitly rather than assuming.

BIs frozen encoder + PPO defensible given the RL/driving literature?Partial — with a live contradiction
Defensible as a research question. Not defensible as an assumed-standard practice, because in driving it is not standard at all.

The paradigm is established in control broadly: Parisi et al. detach a frozen encoder from the policy and find it competitive with ground-truth state features, while end-to-end from scratch fails at the same data budget. That is real support.

But in CARLA specifically, the two strongest RL results go the other way. Roach trains PPO on a privileged BEV and distils down to a camera student. CaRL trains its convolutional encoder end-to-end with PPO on a privileged BEV raster, at 300 M samples. Neither runs PPO on frozen learned sensor features. So the honest framing is: "we test whether the frozen-pre-trained-representation paradigm, established in manipulation and navigation, transfers to closed-loop driving." That is a legitimate contribution and a better story than pretending it is the default.

Before relying on Parisi, needs check the counter-literature arguing learning-from-scratch baselines are competitive with pre-trained representations. If that holds up, it weakens the paradigm claim and should appear in the limitations.

CIs IL waypoints → frozen representation → PPO residual precedented?Novel as a combination
Each link has precedent. The chain does not.

IL waypoints from expert demonstrations: direct (TransFuser, LBC). Frozen representation feeding a downstream decision module: direct in driving (LMDrive freezes its vision encoder for the LLM) and partial for policies (Parisi). RL as a bounded residual on a nominal controller: needs check — the residual-RL citations were not opened this session and Decision 2.1 rests on them.

The specific composition — imitation-pretrain a waypoint head, freeze it, and let PPO emit a bounded lateral/speed correction in trajectory space — NOVEL PROPOSAL — NO DIRECT PRECEDENT FOUND in driving. Note also that Roach explicitly argues against PID trajectory tracking ("not reliable … requires excessive parameter tuning"), which is a direct objection to the execution half of our design and should be answered rather than ignored.

DIs the DrivingIntent seam supported by existing systems?Partial
Yes, better than expected — InterFuser is close to a direct precedent.

InterFuser produces an interpretable intermediate — a "safety mind map" carrying surrounding objects and traffic signs — and uses it as a safety heuristic that constrains actions within a safe set. That is structurally the typed-intermediate-record idea: a legible, inspectable interface between perception and control, used for more than a latent hand-off. DriveVLM-Dual's fast/slow split is the second precedent. LMDrive's instruction-completion flag is a third element of the proposed record that already exists in the literature.

Our version differs in being typed and ablatable by construction, with a scripted-oracle implementation as the first adapter. Since the spec already defers this past the ladder, no change is needed — but when it returns, InterFuser is the design to build on, and the oracle-first approach appears to be ours.

EIs Perceiver-style cross-attention right, or is something better established?Partial — recommend a change
Query-based attention is well established in driving. But for RGB+LiDAR specifically, multi-resolution fusion is better validated than our single late stage.

Learned queries over multimodal driving tokens: solidly precedented (InterFuser's transformer decoder queries, LMDrive's Q-Former with learnable queries producing BEV / waypoint / traffic-light tokens). That part of Decision 5.1 stands.

What does not stand is the implicit claim that a single late fusion stage is sufficient. TransFuser's core contribution is fusing "at multiple resolutions" with positional and velocity embeddings, and that is the paper with the cleanest RGB+LiDAR ablation. Fusing once, late, over pooled tokens risks a null R3 that is ambiguous between "LiDAR doesn't help here" and "we fused too late". Recommendation: add a multi-resolution fusion variant to the R5 family and decide it offline on waypoint ADE.

Also worth softening: the earlier audit's claim that mean-pooling is a major flaw. TransFuser average-pools to a 64-d vector and was state of the art. Queries are better justified for referring expressions — a language argument — than for perception in general.

FIs language genuinely useful when a nav command already exists?Partial — and this should reshape R4
For navigation content, probably not. For notice content, yes — and that distinction is visible in how LMDrive built its benchmark.

LangAuto deliberately gives the agent only language: "our benchmark only provides the AV with a navigation instruction and optional notice instructions", explicitly contrasted with Town05 and Longest6 which supply discrete commands or target waypoints. The design implies the authors did not consider language-plus-command a measurable setting for navigation.

No paper in the verified set demonstrates that language adds navigational value on top of an intact nav command. What LMDrive does show is a separate Notice track, where the instruction carries hazard information no nav channel encodes. So: move the centre of gravity of R4 from the junction case to the notice case. The junction case remains as a secondary condition, but if it is the headline, the honest expected result is null.

GDoes the hazard-instruction experiment have precedent?Direct — downgrade our novelty claim
Yes. LMDrive's LangAuto-Notice track is exactly this, and the spec overclaimed.

The Perception Ladder Spec called IC-5 "the cleanest possible language result" and implied it was a new experimental scenario. It is not: notice instructions are a first-class category in the LMDrive dataset ("Please watch out for the pedestrians up ahead"), and LangAuto-Notice is a dedicated evaluation track.

This is good news, not bad. It means the experiment is validated rather than speculative, we can adopt their track structure and metrics directly, and we should position ours as a controlled replication at ~1/300th the model scale — which is itself an interesting claim, since it asks whether notice-instruction following requires an LLM at all. Rewrite the IC-5 section to cite LangAuto-Notice.

HDoes the edge-distillation strategy have precedent?Partial — the half that matters is supported
Latent-feature distillation in CARLA is directly precedented. Reusing a frozen RL policy across the distillation is not.

Roach states plainly that it supervises the student with "action distributions, value estimations and latent features", and cites SAM for matching latent features between a proxy expert and an end-to-end agent. LBC is the broader two-stage precedent. So "train a small encoder to match a bigger encoder's latent, in CARLA" is established practice.

What is not precedented is the consequence we drew from the fixed-latent invariant: distil only the encoder, keep the PPO policy head bit-identical, and skip RL re-training entirely. NOVEL PROPOSAL — NO DIRECT PRECEDENT FOUND. It is a clean idea and follows logically, but it needs its own validation — measure driving score before and after distillation with the policy untouched, and report the gap.

2 · Partially supported — precedent exists, our combination differs

  • Frozen encoder + PPO. Established in manipulation/navigation (Parisi); contradicted in driving by Roach and CaRL. Reframe as the research question, not the default.
  • Single late fusion for RGB+LiDAR. TransFuser's validated design is multi-resolution. Add the variant to R5.
  • Partial nav-command suppression. LangAuto removes the command entirely. Ours is weaker but more controlled — state it.
  • Time-conditioned waypoints only. CarLLaVA documents that waypoints handle turns poorly and proposes a semi-disentangled path + waypoint output.
  • Perturbation-based recovery in sensor space. ChauffeurNet synthesises in a mid-level BEV and explicitly flags raw-sensor perturbation as the hard case.

3 · Novel — no direct precedent found

  • Fixed latent width + parameter matching as the explicit control for a perception-richness ladder. The single cleanest methodological contribution here.
  • IL waypoint head → frozen representation → bounded PPO residual in trajectory space, as a composed pipeline in driving.
  • Encoder-only distillation with a bit-identical frozen RL policy head, avoiding RL re-training for the edge model.
  • The counterfactual instruction triad (correct / shuffled / null on one trained policy) as the primary language statistic.
  • A blind rung with a numeric threshold as a formal leakage gate.

4 · Should be dropped or corrected

  • drop Citing TransFuser for multi-view fusion or for a shared RGB/BEV tokeniser. It stitches three cameras into one image and uses separate backbones. The legacy documents got this wrong; do not inherit it.
  • drop Claiming IC-5 as a novel experiment. LangAuto-Notice exists. Cite it and reposition as a small-scale controlled replication.
  • drop The 1.5 M-step PPO budget. Two orders of magnitude below CaRL, seven times below Roach's privileged-BEV policy.
  • drop The product-of-three-factors reward. Replace with CaRL's structure, which was designed precisely because PPO fails to optimise complex rewards at large mini-batch sizes.
  • drop Autopilot as the Stage 0 expert. Roach is trained via RL and outperforms the CARLA autopilot; students trained on it do better. Use a privileged coach instead.
  • soften "Mean-pooling is a major architectural flaw." TransFuser pools to 64-d and was SOTA. Keep queries, but justify them by referring expressions rather than by pooling being broken.
  • correct Single max-height LiDAR BEV. TransFuser's 2-bin above/below-ground histogram is the validated representation.

5 · Needs experimental validation before it can be claimed

  • That a frozen sensor encoder + PPO reaches useful driving performance in CARLA at all — this is the project's core bet and it runs against the driving literature.
  • That the residual action space gives PPO enough authority to matter (ΔDS per rung).
  • That a fixed 128-d latent is not itself the binding constraint at the richest rung (width sweep 64/128/256).
  • That R1's FOV leaves headroom for R2 to measure anything.
  • That single late fusion does not under-read LiDAR relative to multi-resolution fusion.
  • That encoder-only distillation preserves driving score with the policy head untouched.
  • That instructions generalise across held-out templates, not just paraphrases.

6 · Revised architecture

Nine changes, all justified by a verified source. Nothing else in the spec changes.

  1. Add a privileged PPO coach as the Stage 0 expert and the performance ceiling

    A Roach/CaRL-style PPO policy on a privileged BEV raster. Three things at once: it generates better IL data than the autopilot (Roach's central result), it establishes the ceiling the ladder is climbing toward, and it tells you whether a flat ladder is a perception problem or an RL problem. It does not violate your constraint — the ladder's downstream learner is still PPO on the frozen latent; the coach is a data generator and a reference point.

  2. Adopt CaRL's reward structure

    Route completion as the single primary term, soft penalties multiplicatively reducing it, hard infractions terminating the episode with a terminal penalty. CaRL's finding — that PPO fails to optimise complex rewards as mini-batch size grows — is directly relevant to a design that wants parallel environments.

  3. Adopt a Beta action distribution and Roach's exploration loss

    Roach's ablation shows Gaussian PPO gets "trapped in a local minimum where staying still is the most rewarding strategy" — precisely the degenerate behaviour the earlier audit found the legacy success metric rewarding. Bounded support, no tanh squashing, explicit KL. Cheap to adopt, well validated.

  4. Re-budget PPO, and calibrate with a pilot

    Run one rung at several budgets and plot the learning curve before fixing the ladder's number. The residual formulation genuinely should need far fewer samples than from-scratch control — but "should" is a hypothesis, and 1.5 M was chosen from wall-clock, not from evidence.

  5. Pre-register R1's camera FOV at ~60–70°

    Otherwise a 125° front camera pre-empts the side cameras and R2 measures nothing. This one decision determines whether question B is answerable.

  6. Switch the LiDAR BEV to a 2-bin height histogram, and keep the goal out of it

    TransFuser's representation, minus their rasterised goal channel — which would confound R3 by putting navigation information into the LiDAR branch.

  7. Add multi-resolution fusion to the R5 family

    Decided offline on waypoint ADE. Without it, a null R3 is ambiguous between "LiDAR doesn't help" and "we fused too late".

  8. Adopt CarLLaVA's semi-disentangled output

    Space-conditioned path waypoints for lateral control plus time-conditioned waypoints for speed. Addresses the documented weakness of pure waypoints in turns, from the entry that won CARLA Challenge 2.0.

  9. Re-centre R4 on notice instructions, and add a misleading-instruction condition

    Junction disambiguation becomes secondary. Add LMDrive's misleading-instruction case to the C2 triad — an agent that follows an unsafe instruction has learned the wrong thing, and it is a cheap, strong test.

6 · Is this a sensible research direction?

Verdict

Yes — the components belong together, with one honest caveat and one real risk. This is not a pile of unrelated parts. Multi-view + LiDAR + query-based fusion + waypoint output + auxiliary pretraining + frozen encoder is, almost exactly, the InterFuser/LMDrive/TransFuser consensus architecture. You are not inventing a stack; you are building the well-validated one at a smaller scale and ablating it more carefully than the original papers did. That is a legitimate and useful contribution — the perception ablations in these papers are thin, and none of them controls for parameter count.

The caveat is where PPO sits. Every strong CARLA RL result puts RL upstream on privileged state and imitation downstream on sensors. Your constraint puts PPO downstream of frozen sensor features, which is validated in manipulation and navigation but not in driving. That is not a reason to abandon it — it is a reason to name it as the contribution: "does the frozen-pre-trained-representation paradigm transfer to closed-loop driving?" Framed that way it is interesting and the ladder is exactly the right instrument. Framed as an obvious engineering choice, the first reviewer who knows Roach will ask why you did not do it the other way, and you need an answer better than "we preferred it".

The real risk is compute, and it is quantitative rather than conceptual. CaRL needed 300 M samples on 8×A100 for 64 DS. Roach needed 10 M for a privileged policy. You proposed 1.5 M on one Windows machine. The residual formulation is a genuine mitigation — correcting a competent trajectory is a far smaller learning problem than learning control from scratch — but it is untested, and if it fails, the ladder returns flat differences at every rung and you will not be able to tell whether perception did not help or PPO did not learn. The pilot in revision 4 exists to find this out in days rather than months, and the privileged coach in revision 1 exists so that a flat ladder is still diagnosable.

Where the specification was genuinely wrong, and this audit corrected it: TransFuser does not do multi-view fusion or shared RGB/BEV tokenisation; the hazard-instruction experiment already exists as LangAuto-Notice; mean-pooling is not the flaw the earlier audit claimed; the autopilot is a weaker expert than an RL coach; and the sample budget was off by two orders of magnitude. None of those is fatal. All five were cheap to find now and expensive to find later.

What is genuinely yours is the experimental method, not the architecture — fixed latent width plus matched parameters as a control, the blind rung as a leakage gate, the counterfactual instruction triad, and encoder-only distillation with a frozen policy. That is a reasonable thing for this project to contribute, and it is more defensible than any architectural novelty claim would have been.
