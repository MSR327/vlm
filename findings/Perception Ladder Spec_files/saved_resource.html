Perception Ladder Spec

A controlled experimental design in which perception is the independent variable and PPO is held fixed — so that each rung's gain is attributable to exactly one thing.

Does progressively richer multimodal perception and language-conditioned reasoning improve autonomous driving when the downstream PPO policy is kept consistent?

0 · The invariants

Everything in this document follows from one idea. The research question asks whether richer perception helps with PPO held constant. That phrasing turns PPO from a component into a measuring instrument. An instrument is only useful if it does not change between measurements — so the first job of the specification is to nail down every variable that is not perception.

These twelve quantities are identical in every experiment on the ladder. Any rung that violates one is not comparable and does not count.

Decision 0.1
The encoder is frozen during PPO in every rung

After representation pretraining, encoder and waypoint head are frozen. PPO trains only the actor-critic — roughly 140 K parameters, none of them perceptual.

Why

It is what makes the question answerable. If the encoder adapts during RL, "richer perception helped" becomes inseparable from "richer perception adapted more easily under RL". Freezing makes perception the independent variable and PPO the constant — literally the phrasing of the research question.

Rejected

End-to-end PPO through the encoder. High-variance policy gradients over ~4 K-sample batches will degrade features learned from 500 K supervised frames, and the degradation will differ per rung — destroying comparability along with the features.

Validated by

X2 (§3), run once on the winning rung only, after the ladder concludes: unfreeze the adapter at LR/10 and measure whether driving score moves.

A consequence you should accept explicitly

With a frozen encoder, a frozen waypoint head and a 2-d residual action, PPO's authority over the outcome is deliberately narrow. That is the price of using it as an instrument. To keep PPO's own contribution visible, every rung is evaluated twice: once with the residual forced to zero (R_k^IL, no RL at all) and once with the trained policy (R_k^PPO). The quantity ΔDS = DS(R_k^PPO) − DS(R_k^IL) is free to compute and directly answers a question your framing implies but does not state: does PPO's contribution grow or shrink as perception gets richer?

Decision 1.1
A sparse, noisy navigation target is allowed; dense lane geometry is not

The line between "navigation" and "leakage" is not about whether the map was consulted — it is about whether the quantity is the answer the perception is supposed to compute.

Why

A real car has a nav system, and every CARLA Leaderboard agent receives a sparse GPS route plus a high-level command. Withholding it means the agent has no destination, which is why the previous design could not measure route completion at all. The 25–40 m sampling distance and the 1 m noise are what keep it a hint rather than a target to servo onto.

Rejected

Dense lane-centre distance and heading error (the previous leak), and a target point closer than 20 m. Both let a controller drive by pursuing the nav signal alone, with perception unused — exactly the failure the audit found.

Validated by

Rung R0. If a blind policy with only ego + nav reaches a competitive driving score, the target point is still too informative and its distance must increase until R0 collapses.

Tier P — reward and evaluation only privileged

Available to the reward function, the metric logger and the offline label generator. Never to the policy, and never to any module in the policy's forward path.

  • Lane-centre distance, heading error, current lane id and type, next-junction geometry
  • Collision events, lane-invasion events, red-light-violation events, off-road state
  • Route completion fraction, distance-along-route, blocked-timer
  • Ground-truth actor list: positions, velocities, bounding boxes, types, colours, traffic-light states
  • Expert (autopilot / privileged planner) future trajectory
  • Semantic segmentation and depth ground truth
The rule that makes Tier P usable

Privileged information may be a training label. It may never be a deployed input. This is the Learning-by-Cheating pattern and it is entirely standard: an auxiliary head that predicts traffic-light state from pixels is supervised by ground truth, but at inference the prediction comes from the camera. The distinction is mechanical — trace whether the quantity appears in the forward pass at evaluation time. If it does, it is leakage; if it only appears in a loss term during Stage 1, it is supervision.

Tier F — forbidden outright never

Not merely "not in the policy" — not anywhere in the pipeline, including as a label.

  • Any Tier-P quantity entering the policy forward pass at evaluation time
  • The full future route beyond the single sampled target point
  • Other agents' future trajectories or intentions (the expert's own future is a Tier-P label; other actors' futures are not available at any stage)
  • The episode seed, route id, town id, or weather id as a model input — these enable trivial memorisation and would silently invalidate every generalisation test
  • Evaluation-set statistics of any kind in the normalisation constants
  • Any reward-derived signal fed back as an observation
Automated leak test — a gate, not a suggestion

Before any rung above R1 is trained, run the blind control R0 to completion. If DS(R0) ≥ 0.6 × DS(R1), the contract is leaking and the ladder is halted until the source is found. This test costs one training run and is the only thing standing between you and a semester of uninterpretable results. Re-run it whenever the observation contract changes.

2 · PPO interface

What enters

ppo_obs = [ z_t (128) ] # encoder latent — the ONLY thing that varies by rung [ ego_t (8) ] # speed, yaw_rate, a_lon, a_lat, prev_action(2), speed_hist(2) [ nav_t (8) ] # target_point(2), command one-hot(6) [ tau_nom (8) ] # nominal trajectory from the frozen IL head, 4 waypoints × (x,y) = 152-d, identical in every rung

Rung R0 has no sensors, but it still has an "encoder": a 2-layer MLP over [ego, nav] that outputs 128 dimensions. This keeps the PPO input literally identical in shape and in normalisation, so R0 is a true control rather than a differently-shaped model.

What PPO outputs

action = [ delta_lat in [-1, 1] ] # lateral shift of the nominal trajectory, x 1.5 m [ delta_v in [-1, 1] ] # target-speed scale, v_target * (1 + 0.4 * delta_v) execution: tau_exec = shift_lateral(tau_nom, 1.5 * delta_lat) v_exec = v_nom * (1 + 0.4 * delta_v) steer = pure_pursuit(tau_exec, ego.speed) throttle, brake = pi_longitudinal(v_exec, ego.speed)
Decision 2.1
PPO receives a compact latent and outputs a bounded residual in trajectory space

Not raw multimodal features, not waypoints-as-observation, not direct controls.

Why

A fixed-width latent is the only PPO input that can stay identical while the encoder behind it grows from an MLP to a multimodal transformer. A bounded residual around a competent nominal trajectory means exploration starts from sane driving rather than random flailing, keeps the action space 2-d and identically-scaled in every rung, and inherits kinematic feasibility from the controller instead of approximating it with an EMA.

Rejected

Raw features — dimension varies per rung, which is the confound the whole design exists to avoid. Waypoints as PPO's action — a 2K-dim action space makes exploration intractable at this sample budget. Direct controls — discards the IL prior, so PPO restarts from scratch in every rung and the sample budget dominates the perception effect.

Validated by

X1 (§3): the same rung with PPO emitting [steer, longitudinal] directly and no trajectory intermediate. If X1 matches or beats the residual formulation at equal budget, this decision is wrong and the ladder should be re-run in the direct-control interface.

Fixed PPO configuration

parallel envs 8 CARLA instances (drop to 4 if VRAM-bound; keep constant across rungs) rollout 512 steps per env -> 4096-sample batch epochs 6 minibatches 8 optimiser Adam, lr 3e-4, linear decay to 0 gamma 0.99 gae_lambda 0.95 clip 0.2 vf_coef 0.5 ent_coef 0.005 grad_norm 0.5 log_std state-independent, learnable, init log(0.3) total 1.5 M env steps ~= 5-6 h wall-clock at 4 envs advantage normalised per batch; value loss clipped

Every departure from the current implementation is deliberate: parallel environments (single-env PPO cannot reach 1.5 M steps in reasonable time), proper minibatching (BATCH_SIZE is currently never read), 6 epochs rather than 15 (15 over-fits a 4 K batch), and updates on rollout boundaries rather than a wall-clock timestep counter.

Not on the ladder, and why

Weather ablations (weather is an evaluation axis — training a rung per weather answers nothing about perception). Reward tuning studies (the reward is an invariant; tuning it mid-ladder invalidates every completed rung). Pedestrian-density ablations (a scenario property, not a perception treatment). Resolution sweeps (a hyperparameter, decided once offline against the edge budget). Every one of these is a real question; none of them is this question.

Compute budget — check this before committing

6 rungs + 2 surviving fusion variants + C3 + X1 = 10 configurations × 3 seeds = 30 PPO runs. At 4 parallel CARLA envs and ~80 environment steps/second wall-clock, 1.5 M steps is roughly 5.2 hours per run: ≈ 160 GPU-hours, about 7 days of continuous running, plus Stage 0 collection (~1 day) and Stage 1 offline training (~1–2 days, parallelisable and CARLA-free). Measure your actual throughput on one dry run before committing to 1.5 M steps — if you get half the expected rate, cut to 1.0 M steps uniformly rather than cutting rungs.

4 · The language experiment

The existing hash encoder is discarded entirely. But replacing it with a real text encoder is the easy half. The hard half is designing instructions that carry information the rest of the observation does not — because if the instruction is redundant with nav.command, the policy will learn to ignore it and you will measure nothing while appearing to run a language experiment.

What language provides beyond an 8-way command ID

ClassExampleWhy an ID cannot express it
Object-referential"stop behind the white van"Requires binding a linguistic description to a specific visual entity. No finite command vocabulary enumerates the referents.
Ordinal / compositional"take the second left, then go straight"Multi-step structure over a horizon longer than one junction decision.
Constraint"stay in the right lane through the tunnel"A persistent condition with a spatial scope, not a momentary action.
Speed / style"drive slowly, the road is wet"Modulates a continuous parameter with a stated reason.
Hazard notice"careful, a cyclist is coming from your right"Supplies information the sensors do not yet have. The strongest case — see below.
Negation"don't change lanes here"Prohibitions have no natural encoding in a positive action vocabulary.
The cleanest possible language result

Scenario IC-5: a hazard notice is delivered before the hazard is visible to any sensor — a cyclist behind an occlusion, an emergency vehicle approaching from a side street. At that instant, no amount of perception richness can supply what the sentence supplies. If R4 decelerates earlier or gives more lateral clearance than R3 on these scenarios, that is not a marginal statistical effect: it is a demonstration that language contributed information the sensor stack structurally could not have. Build this scenario family deliberately; it is the strongest evidence the project can produce.

Generating instructions in CARLA

Programmatic generation from privileged state at collection time, then LLM paraphrase for surface diversity. Instructions are inputs from a human passenger or nav system, so generating them from ground truth is legitimate — the constraint is that the instruction may only reference things a passenger could plausibly know or see.

  1. Template instantiation from privileged state

    At each collection frame, CARLA knows the route plan, the actor list with types and colours, lane topology, traffic-light states and weather. Templates draw from these: "{action} at the {ordinal} intersection", "follow the {colour} {type} ahead", "stop behind the {colour} {type}", "stay in the {lane} lane for {distance} metres", "careful, a {actor} is approaching from the {side}". Target ~40 templates across the six classes above.

  2. LLM paraphrase, offline, on the Windows box

    15–20 paraphrases per template, generated once and reviewed by hand for meaning preservation. This is what turns a template set into something a text encoder can generalise over, rather than 40 memorisable strings.

  3. Split on two axes, not one

    Held-out paraphrases of seen templates test surface-form generalisation. Held-out templates — new structures, new referent types — test compositional generalisation. These are different claims and must be reported separately; a system can pass the first and fail the second completely.

  4. Cache every embedding

    The instruction set is finite (≈ 40 × 18 ≈ 700 strings). Encode all of them once with the frozen text encoder and store a lookup table. The text encoder therefore never runs in the control loop, on the training machine or on the edge device — the language pathway costs a 128-float table lookup at inference. This is an honest architecture, not a shortcut, and it is what makes language affordable at the edge.

Decision 4.1
Instruction compliance is made instrumentally necessary, not directly rewarded

The reward function contains no compliance term. Instead, instruction-critical routes are constructed so that ignoring the instruction reduces route progress — and at those junctions, nav.command is suppressed so only the sentence disambiguates the branch.

Why

Three things at once. The reward stays byte-identical across R0–R4, so R4 − R3 is a pure input difference. There is no compliance term to reward-hack. And compliance becomes something the agent needs rather than something it is bribed for, which is a far stronger result.

Rejected

An explicit compliance reward term — it either differs between R3 and R4 (confound) or is present-but-unachievable in R0–R3 (adds uncontrollable noise to their returns). The legacy _compute_speech_reward is also directly hackable: it rewards steering sign, so a policy can comply by swerving.

Validated by

C2, the counterfactual triad. If ICR(correct) − ICR(shuffled) is not clearly positive with a CI excluding zero, the language claim is not supported and the project reframes around R3.

Text encoder

Recommendation: a frozen sentence encoder — MiniLM-L6 (22 M) or the MobileCLIP-S0 text tower — with a trainable 128-d projection. Both are small, both produce genuinely compositional embeddings, and both are cached away at inference. Qwen2-0.5B hidden states are the alternative worth considering, purely because it connects to the existing Aanchal_Spring speech work and would let a later phase run audio → embedding end to end. Choose on one offline test: encode all held-out paraphrases and check that each sits nearest its own template's cluster. That test costs minutes and is decisive.

Terminology, held to

R4 is language-conditioned multimodal perception. It is not a VLM. The term becomes earned only when visual tokens are projected into a language model's embedding space and that language model does the reasoning — the LMDrive or DriveVLM structure. Nothing in this ladder does that, by design: it would break the frozen-PPO invariant and blow the edge budget. If a later phase adds a real VLM as a 2 Hz slow pathway feeding a typed intent record, the word applies then. Use it then and not before.

5 · Fusion and architecture Deliverable C

Comparing the four operators

Decision 5.1
Cross-attention with a fixed set of learned latent queries (Perceiver-style)

M = 32 queries cross-attend over the union of camera tokens, BEV tokens and instruction tokens; 2 self-attention layers among the queries; pooled and projected to the 128-d latent.

Why

It is the only operator whose output width is independent of how many modalities feed it — which is precisely what the ladder's fixed-latent invariant requires. Adding LiDAR adds tokens, not output dimensions. Cost is linear in tokens, so the edge budget survives. Attention maps give you inspectable evidence for the "reasoning helped" claim rather than only a number. And it subsumes concatenation and FiLM, which remain available as R5 ablations.

Rejected

Mean-pooling — the current design's approach, which computes a 1000-token spatial representation and then averages it away. Joint self-attention — quadratic, and the thing that would make the edge target unreachable. FiLM alone — cannot ground referring expressions.

Validated by

The R5 family, decided offline on waypoint ADE/FDE, then confirmed closed-loop on the top two.

Decision 5.2
Camera and BEV stay in separate token sets with modality embeddings — for now

Rather than lifting camera features into a shared BEV frame using known intrinsics and extrinsics.

Why

Geometric lifting (LSS / BEVFormer style) is more correct but is a substantial subsystem, and it is not what the ladder is testing. Learned correspondence via cross-attention is the TransFuser-lite position and is adequate to answer "does LiDAR help".

Rejected

Geometric lifting in this phase. Also rejected: sharing one tokeniser between perspective RGB and top-down BEV, as the legacy code's docstring claims TransFuser does. TransFuser deliberately uses separate backbones because the two frames have incompatible statistics.

Validated by

An optional R3-lift variant after the ladder, offline only: if geometric lifting improves waypoint ADE by a clear margin, it earns a closed-loop run. This is honestly the weakest link in the architecture and should be labelled as such in any write-up.

Yes — waypoints can be pretrained from autopilot and consumed by PPO

This is the mechanism, concretely:

  • Label. At collection frame t, record the expert's future poses at t+0.5, +1.0, +1.5, +2.0 s and transform them into the ego frame at time t. Four (x, y) pairs. The transform is exact, so labels are noise-free.
  • Train. Stage 1 trains encoder + waypoint head with L1 loss on those four points, alongside the auxiliary heads. This is a supervised regression problem over ~400 K frames — it converges in hours and needs no simulator.
  • Freeze. Both encoder and waypoint head are frozen for Stage 3. At every control step the head emits τ_nom, which is also fed to PPO as 8 of its 152 input dimensions, so the policy knows what it is correcting.
  • Correct. PPO's δ_lat shifts τ_nom laterally by up to ±1.5 m and δ_v scales the target speed by up to ±40%. Pure-pursuit and a PI loop turn that into controls.

The design consequence worth stating plainly: PPO's ceiling is bounded by what a ±1.5 m lateral shift and a ±40% speed change can repair. It can slow for a hazard the expert did not slow for, nudge away from a drifting vehicle, and recover from moderate lateral error. It cannot invent a different route or execute an unplanned lane change. If the ladder shows PPO adding almost nothing in any rung, widen the residual bounds before concluding that RL does not help — and report that you did.

Covariate shift — the thing that quietly ruins IL-pretrained waypoints

Behaviour cloning on clean autopilot data produces a head that is excellent on-distribution and useless once the car drifts, because it never saw a drifted state. Mitigate at collection time, not later: during ~20% of frames, inject a bounded lateral or heading perturbation into the ego vehicle, then record the expert's recovery trajectory as the label. This gives recovery supervision at a fraction of DAgger's cost and no extra training loop. Without it, PPO's ±1.5 m residual will spend its entire budget compensating for a fragile nominal trajectory.

7 · Training pipeline Deliverable D

  1. Stage 0 — Collection (CARLA, once)

    Privileged expert or tuned autopilot on Town01 + Town03 + Town04, ~400 K frames across 6 weather presets, with 20% perturbation-recovery frames. Store raw, not derived: raw LiDAR points (not a rasterised BEV), the full route (not just the sampled target), and all Tier-P state. You will change the BEV representation and the target-point distance at least once; re-collecting is the expensive mistake.

    Instructions are generated and stored per frame at this stage. Trainable: nothing.

  2. Stage 1 — Representation pretraining (offline, no CARLA)

    Encoder + waypoint head + auxiliary heads, trained by supervised learning on the Stage 0 dataset. Losses: waypoint L1 (primary), traffic-light-state cross-entropy, BEV drivable-area/occupancy BCE, and — in R4 — instruction-completion BCE.

    Frozen: text encoder (always), and the pretrained vision backbone if one is used. Trainable: tokenisers, latent queries, cross-attention, projection, all heads. Split by route and weather, never by shuffled row index — driving frames are temporally correlated and a random split leaks neighbouring frames into validation.

  3. Stage 2 — Offline architecture selection (no CARLA)

    The R5 fusion family and any resolution or query-count choices are decided here, on waypoint ADE/FDE and auxiliary-head accuracy. Cheap, fast, parallelisable, and it prunes the closed-loop matrix from four fusion variants to two. Frozen/trainable as Stage 1.

  4. Stage 3 — PPO (CARLA, the ladder)

    Frozen: encoder, waypoint head, auxiliary heads, text encoder, normalisation statistics. Trainable: actor-critic only, ~140 K parameters. Identical configuration and seeds in every rung. This stage is the ladder.

  5. Stage 4 — Partial fine-tuning · outside the ladder

    Unfreeze the last cross-attention block at LR/10 on the winning rung only, after all ladder results are recorded. Report separately.

    This is the stage to be suspicious of. It breaks the frozen-encoder invariant by construction, so its results cannot be compared to any rung — and a common outcome is a modest closed-loop gain accompanied by a collapse in waypoint ADE, meaning the representation was traded for reward-specific overfitting. Run it as a curiosity with a clearly-labelled result, or skip it. Do not let it into the main comparison.

8 · Edge constraint and deployment path Deliverable F

The constraint has to shape the research model now, or the compression phase becomes a rewrite. Two things make this tractable: the policy is trivially small (~140 K parameters — never the bottleneck), and the text encoder never runs online (§4). Essentially the entire edge budget is the visual encoder.

Say this out loud in the thesis

Four cameras plus LiDAR plus language at 20 Hz on a Raspberry Pi CPU is not achievable, and no amount of quantisation changes that. Either the edge target is a Jetson-class device with an accelerator, or the deployed configuration is a reduced sensor set and the thesis reports the accuracy cost of that reduction — which is itself a legitimate and interesting result. What is not acceptable is projecting a Pi latency number that no measurement supports, as the legacy design documents do.

Path from research model to device

  1. Architectural slimming

    Reduce latent queries 32 → 16, drop one self-attention layer, halve input resolution, drop the second temporal frame. Re-run Stage 1 offline and read the ADE cost of each cut. All decisions offline, no CARLA.

  2. Distil the encoder, keep the policy

    Train the slim encoder to match the research encoder's 128-d latent (cosine + MSE) and its waypoint outputs, on the Stage 0 dataset. Because the latent is matched, the frozen PPO policy transfers unchanged — no RL re-training is needed for the edge model. This is the single most valuable consequence of the fixed-latent invariant, and it is worth stating as a design contribution.

  3. Quantise

    Post-training INT8 with a 2 K-frame calibration set drawn from the training distribution. If driving score drops more than 2 points, escalate to quantisation-aware training. Measure the drop on the closed-loop metric, not on ADE — they diverge.

  4. Export and accelerate

    TorchScript → ONNX → TensorRT (Jetson) or ONNX Runtime / ExecuTorch (Pi). The legacy TF-Lite path in convert.py targets the old TensorFlow VAE and is not reusable.

  5. Measure honestly on the device

    p50/p95/p99 broken out per stage — preprocessing, encoder, policy, controller — with real sensor decoding included, not synthetic tensors. Then a closed-loop processor-in-the-loop run reusing the existing PIL harness, reporting driving score at the device's achieved control rate, since latency degrades driving and that degradation is the actual finding.

Attributing a gain to the right cause

A number going up is not a finding. The design that turns it into one is pre-registration of which sub-metric each rung should move, written down before the run.

Statistics — decide before you run

3 seeds minimum, 5 preferred. ≥ 30 evaluation routes per condition — CARLA driving score has enough route-to-route variance that fewer will not separate rungs. Report mean ± std across seeds and a bootstrap CI over routes. Pre-register the primary metric (DS on G2) and the primary language metric (ΔICR) in writing before the first run. The ladder has enough numbers in it that post-hoc selection of a favourable one is very easy and completely invalidating.

10 · The first experiment Deliverable G

E-0: the protocol harness plus rungs R0 and R1, parameter-matched, 3 seeds. Nothing about VLMs, LiDAR, or language. Four steps, with a hard gate at the end.

  1. Build the harness — no learning at all

    The two-record environment (SensorObs / PrivilegedState with the enforced signature), the route set (40 train / 30 eval), the weather schedule, the reward, the full metric pipeline, seeding, and the parallel-env wrapper. Validate it with a scripted controller: a pure-pursuit agent driven by the privileged route should score near-perfectly, and the same controller with the route withheld should fail. That pair of runs proves the harness measures driving.

  2. Stage 0 collection on Town01

    ~400 K frames with recovery perturbations, raw LiDAR, full Tier-P state. The long pole in wall-clock, and everything downstream depends on getting the stored schema right. Review the schema before starting the run.

  3. Offline Stage 1 for R0 and R1

    Waypoint ADE/FDE for both. No CARLA. First real signal — and if R1's ADE is not clearly better than R0's, the vision path has a bug and you find out in hours rather than after a week of RL.

  4. PPO for R0 and R1, 3 seeds each

    Six runs, ~30 hours. Produces the first driving scores and exercises the whole pipeline end to end.

Gate — do not build R2 before this passes
  • DS(R0) < 0.6 × DS(R1) — otherwise the observation contract is still leaking, and every rung above would be measuring the leak.
  • DS(R1) meaningfully > DS(R0), with the gap larger than the seed spread — otherwise the environment does not reward perception, and adding sensors cannot possibly help.
  • ΔDS = DS(R1PPO) − DS(R1IL) > 0 — otherwise PPO's residual authority is too narrow to matter and the bounds need widening before the ladder runs.
  • Seed spread small enough that a plausible rung-to-rung effect would be visible above it. If not, more seeds or more evaluation routes, decided now rather than after ten rungs.

Every one of these four failure modes is cheap to fix at this point and enormously expensive to discover at R4.

11 · Risks and decisions still open

Risks I would put in the plan

  • Throughput. The 7-day estimate assumes ~80 environment steps/second with 4 CARLA instances rendering 4 cameras each. Measure it on one dry run. If it is half that, cut steps uniformly — never cut rungs.
  • Template artifacts. The policy may learn instruction templates rather than language. The paraphrase and template splits (G4/G5) are the mitigation, but this is the most likely way the language result turns out hollow, and it should be named as a limitation regardless of the outcome.
  • Residual ceiling. If PPO adds nothing anywhere, that is a finding about the interface, not about RL. The X1 control and widened bounds are the response.
  • Camera/BEV correspondence is learned, not given (Decision 5.2). This is the architecturally weakest point in the design and should be stated as such.
  • Everything is simulation. No sim-to-real claim is supported by any of this, and the edge deployment demonstrates latency feasibility, not real-world driving.

Decisions I need from you before this becomes implementable

  • CARLA version. 0.9.15 with Leaderboard-2.0-style routes is my recommendation. 0.9.10.1 only if comparability with LMDrive's published numbers matters to you. This choice affects the route API, the traffic manager, and LiDAR semantics, and changing it later invalidates collected data.
  • Edge target device. Jetson-class or Raspberry Pi? §8 has two entirely different budgets and this decision constrains the research model's size now.
  • Is the speech pathway in scope? If Aanchal_Spring must be integrated, the text encoder should be Qwen2-0.5B for continuity, and an audio→instruction rung joins the ladder. If not, MiniLM is the better choice.
  • Seeds: 3 or 5? Five is a 66% compute increase and materially strengthens every claim. Worth deciding against the wall-clock budget rather than by default.
  • Which towns for Stage 0? Town01-only is faster and matches the legacy setup; Town01+03+04 gives a much better representation and makes G2 a stronger result.
