\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Srishanth MSR}

\begin{abstract}
Deploying vision-based Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies onto safety-critical autonomous vehicles requires simultaneous resilience against high-dimensional representation collapse, adversarial observation perturbations, and strict physical embedded execution deadlines. In this work, we present \textbf{SecureDrive}, a comprehensive cyber-physical evaluation framework benchmarking six continuous control DRL formulations across both high-fidelity CARLA simulation and physical Processor-in-the-Loop (PIL) edge hardware (Raspberry Pi 4 Model B, ARM Cortex-A72). We first examine an empirical four-stage sensor ladder (Monocular 100-d, 3-Camera 290-d, 4-Camera 385-d, and Camera-LiDAR BEV 195-d), exposing that naive latent concatenation induces representation collapse, severe observation scale disparity ($\sigma \approx 11\text{--}87$ vs. $O(1)$), and policy standstill traps. We then formulate a rigorous Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) metrics registry capturing effective control frequency ($f_{\text{ctrl}}$), emergency hazard brake latency ($T_{\text{brake}}$), root mean square lane deviation ($d_{\text{RMS}}$), unsafe failure rate ($SF\%$), reward sensitivity ($\Delta R_\%$), 95-dimensional feature attribution ($S_i$), and execution jitter ($\sigma^2_{\text{jitter}}$). Under zeroth-order adversarial attacks (SG-ZO), we benchmark six DRL variants: Vanilla PPO, SA-PPO Core, WCA-PPO, Smoothed DRL (S-PPO), RRL-SG, and our Proposed Action-Smoothed Robust PPO (ASR-PPO). Across 1,200 evaluation episodes and hardware profiling, we uncover a critical cyber-physical dilemma: while inference-time randomized smoothing (S-PPO) guarantees strong certified robustness ($\Delta R_\% = 0.52\%$), its sequential Monte Carlo passes inflate edge latency to $245.3\text{ ms}$ ($3.1\text{ Hz}$), causing dangerous emergency braking delay ($T_{\text{brake}} = 322.6\text{ ms}$). Conversely, our proposed INT8-quantized ASR-PPO policy couples Lipschitz action regularization with multi-step Madry-PGD bounds, achieving $0.0\%$ unsafe failure rate, $1134.10$ attacked return, and deterministic $8.4\text{ Hz}$ edge closed-loop turnaround ($118.45\text{ ms}$ brake response, $3.24\text{ ms}^2$ jitter), resolving the trade-off between adversarial robustness and real-time automotive execution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

End-to-end autonomous driving aims to synthesize continuous steering, throttle, and brake controls directly from onboard perceptual sensor streams~\cite{pomerleau1988, bojarski2016, codevilla2018}. While deep reinforcement learning (DRL) provides a principled framework for learning closed-loop driving policies through environmental interactions~\cite{carla2017, toromanoff2020}, deploying learned policies in physical cyber-physical systems (CPS) introduces stringent requirements that extend beyond idealized nominal rewards~\cite{he2024trustworthy}.

First, urban navigation requires rich spatial perception across multiple cameras and active 3D LiDAR sensors~\cite{transfuser2021, interfuser2022}. However, naive sensor scaling frequently causes \textit{representation collapse} and optimization instability in continuous actor-critic algorithms~\cite{schulman2017}. High-dimensional observation spaces dilute policy gradients, while unnormalized latent representations saturate policy activation layers, driving agents into trivial standstill local optima.

Second, real-world vision pipelines are fundamentally exposed to sensor degradation, camera lens soiling, and malicious adversarial observation perturbations~\cite{zhang2020sa}. A small, bounded perturbation injected into the perceptual state can induce steering chattering, catastrophic lane departure, or collision.

Third, and most critically, autonomous driving is governed by hard cyber-physical latency bounds~\cite{lin2024robust}. Onboard Electronic Control Units (ECUs) and embedded System-on-Chips (SoCs) possess strictly bounded compute budgets (e.g., $1.5\text{ GHz}$ ARM Cortex CPUs). Complex theoretical defense mechanisms---such as inference-time randomized smoothing~\cite{sun2024breaking, kumar2022certifying} or large-scale formal verification solvers---require repetitive sampling or costly inner loops that severely inflate edge inference latency, dropping control frequencies below safety thresholds ($<10\text{ Hz}$) and delaying emergency hazard braking ($T_{\text{brake}}$).

To address these interconnected challenges, we present \textbf{SecureDrive}, a unified cyber-physical evaluation framework for robust deep reinforcement learning in autonomous driving. Our core contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Empirical Sensor Scaling Ladder \& Collapse Analysis:} We evaluate four systematic sensory configurations in CARLA (Monocular 100-d, 3-Cam Forward 290-d, 4-Cam Panoramic 385-d, and Cam+LiDAR BEV 195-d), isolating the numerical and structural failure modes of flat VAE latent concatenation, including observation scale mismatches and standstill reward exploitation.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive CPS Metrics Registry:} We establish a standardized cyber-physical metrics suite evaluating real-time control frequency ($f_{\text{ctrl}}$), emergency hazard brake latency ($T_{\text{brake}}$), cross-track error ($d_{\text{RMS}}$), peak excursion ($d_{\max}$), unsafe failure rate ($SF\%$), reward sensitivity ($\Delta R_\%$), and execution jitter ($\sigma^2_{\text{jitter}}$).
    \item \textbf{6-Variant Robust DRL Benchmark:} We benchmark six continuous PPO formulations under clean and attacked observation manifolds: Vanilla PPO, SA-PPO Core~\cite{zhang2020sa}, WCA-PPO~\cite{liang2022wocar}, S-PPO (Smoothed)~\cite{sun2024breaking}, RRL-SG~\cite{he2024trustworthy}, and our Proposed ASR-PPO.
    \item \textbf{Physical Edge Processor-in-the-Loop (PIL) Deployment:} We deploy and profile all policies on a standalone Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (ARM Cortex-A72) across Server TF-32, Edge FP16, and Edge INT8 quantization over 21,541 closed-loop execution cycles, revealing the critical latency trade-off of smoothed policies.
    \item \textbf{Proposed ASR-PPO Policy:} We develop Action-Smoothed Robust PPO (ASR-PPO), combining temporal Lipschitz action regularizers with multi-step Madry-PGD bounds, achieving $0.0\%$ unsafe failure rate, $1134.10$ attacked return, and deterministic $8.4\text{ Hz}$ edge closed-loop turnaround.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion in Autonomous Driving}
Foundational end-to-end driving architectures relied on single front-facing monocular cameras~\cite{pomerleau1988, bojarski2016, codevilla2018}. To eliminate geometric blind spots at urban intersections, multi-sensor systems integrate surrounding camera arrays with Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) LiDAR projections~\cite{lang2019, bevdet2022}. \textit{Learning by Cheating} (LBC)~\cite{chen2020} demonstrated the efficacy of BEV intermediate representations. \textit{TransFuser}~\cite{transfuser2021} and \textit{InterFuser}~\cite{interfuser2022} pioneered multi-scale transformer fusion, showing that cross-attention between perspective vision and LiDAR grids provides superior scene understanding compared to naive feature concatenation.

\subsection{Robust Deep Reinforcement Learning}
Standard deep reinforcement learning policies are acutely sensitive to observation noise~\cite{schulman2017}. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2020sa} formulated State-Adversarial PPO (SA-PPO) using convex relaxations and SGLD inner optimization to enforce policy robustness within an $\ell_\infty$ ball. Liang et al.~\cite{liang2022wocar} introduced Worst-Case-Aware PPO (WCA-PPO / WocaR), bounding worst-case value degradation without explicit inner-loop adversary optimization. Alternating training paradigms like ATLA~\cite{zhang2021robust} and PA-ATLA~\cite{sun2021policy} train an adversarial policy concurrently to expose policy weaknesses.

\subsection{Certified Defenses \& Randomized Smoothing}
Randomized smoothing~\cite{kumar2022certifying, sun2024breaking} provides provable certified robustness by convolving the policy with Gaussian noise $\mathbf{s} + \boldsymbol{\delta}, \boldsymbol{\delta} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$ and aggregating Monte Carlo forward passes. While mathematically certifiable in discrete action domains, in continuous physical systems, computing smoothed actions requires multiple sequential neural network passes at inference time, imposing severe computational latency penalties.

\subsection{Safe Reinforcement Learning \& Cyber-Physical Control}
Formal safety frameworks such as Responsibility-Sensitive Safety (RSS)~\cite{shalev2017formal} enforce hard mathematical masks over policy actions. He et al.~\cite{he2024trustworthy} proposed RRL-SG, combining safety barrier functions with robust policy optimization. However, executing formal QP solvers or complex safety barriers requires significant compute, typically confining such methods to high-end cloud GPU servers rather than embedded automotive edge nodes.

\section{The SecureDrive Evaluation Framework}

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning Formulation}
We model vehicle navigation as an episodic Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Action Space:} Continuous 2-dimensional vector $\mathbf{a}_t = [a_{\text{steer}}, a_{\text{long}}] \in [-1, 1]^2$. Decoupled longitudinal control guarantees mutually exclusive throttle and braking:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{throttle}_t = \max(a_{\text{long}}, \, 0.0), \quad \text{brake}_t = \max(-a_{\text{long}}, \, 0.0)
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{CAPS Action Smoothing:} To eliminate high-frequency steering jitter on physical actuators, we apply Conditioning for Action Policy Smoothness (CAPS)~\cite{caps2021}:
    \begin{equation}
        \delta_t = 0.6 \, \delta_{t-1} + 0.4 \, a_{\text{steer}, t}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Multi-Objective Reward:} At step $t$, the scalar driving reward is shaped as:
    \begin{equation}
        r_t = R_{\text{speed}} \cdot R_{\text{center}} \cdot R_{\text{angle}} + R_{\text{penalty}}
    \end{equation}
    where $R_{\text{speed}} = \min(v_t / 22.0, 1.0)$, $R_{\text{center}} = \max(1.0 - |d_{\text{center}}| / 3.0, 0.0)$, and $R_{\text{angle}} = \max(1.0 - |\theta_{\text{heading}}| / 20^\circ, 0.0)$. Off-road departure ($|d_{\text{center}}| > 3.0\text{ m}$) or collisions trigger episode termination with penalty $R_{\text{penalty}} = -10.0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Multi-Variant DRL Evaluation Pipeline}
We evaluate six distinct DRL policy formulations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Vanilla PPO Baseline:} Standard unhardened clipped surrogate actor-critic~\cite{schulman2017} trained strictly on clean trajectories:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}^{\text{PPO}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t\right) \right]
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{SA-PPO Core:} State-Adversarial PPO~\cite{zhang2020sa} incorporating bounded observation perturbations via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) inner loops:
    \begin{equation}
        \min_\theta \max_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_\infty \le \epsilon_o} \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_\theta(\cdot \mid \mathbf{s}) \,\|\, \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid \mathbf{s} + \boldsymbol{\delta}))
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{WCA-PPO:} Worst-Case-Aware PPO~\cite{liang2022wocar} bounding policy variance without explicit inner-loop adversary optimization, penalizing the worst-case advantage gap.
    \item \textbf{Smoothed DRL (S-PPO):} Inference-time randomized smoothing~\cite{sun2024breaking, kumar2022certifying} convolving state observations with Gaussian noise:
    \begin{equation}
        \bar{\pi}(\mathbf{s}) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \pi\left(\mathbf{s} + \boldsymbol{\delta}_m\right), \quad \boldsymbol{\delta}_m \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{smooth}}^2 \mathbf{I})
    \end{equation}
    where $M = 8$ Monte Carlo forward passes are computed at each inference step to yield certifiable safety bounds.
    \item \textbf{RRL-SG:} Safe RL architecture~\cite{he2024trustworthy} coupling a nominal PPO policy with a server-side Quadratic Program (QP) enforcing Responsibility-Sensitive Safety (RSS)~\cite{shalev2017formal} kinematic barrier functions.
    \item \textbf{Proposed ASR-PPO:} Our unified Action-Smoothed Robust PPO formulation combining temporal Lipschitz smoothness regularizers with deterministic multi-step Madry-PGD~\cite{madry2018towards} bounds:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{L}^{\text{ASR}}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}^{\text{PPO}}(\theta) - \lambda_{\text{smooth}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}}(\theta) - \lambda_{\text{adv}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}(\theta)
    \end{equation}
    where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{smooth}} = \|\pi_\theta(\mathbf{s}_t) - \pi_\theta(\mathbf{s}_{t-1})\|_2^2$ regularizes actuator chatter, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} = \|\pi_\theta(\mathbf{s}) - \pi_\theta(\mathbf{s} + \boldsymbol{\delta}^*)\|_2^2$ penalizes deviation under the worst-case $K$-step PGD perturbation $\boldsymbol{\delta}^* \in \mathcal{B}_\infty(\epsilon_o)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) Metrics Registry}
To rigorously quantify vehicular control, safety, and real-time computational determinism, we establish a formal CPS metrics registry:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Effective Control Frequency ($f_{\text{ctrl}}$, Hz):} The closed-loop actuation update rate written to the vehicle: $f_{\text{ctrl}} = 1 / t_{\text{loop}}$, where $t_{\text{loop}} = T_{\text{inf}} + T_{\text{rpc}}$.
    \item \textbf{Emergency Hazard Brake Latency ($T_{\text{brake}}$, ms):} The precise temporal duration between an adversarial hazard injection and the actor policy outputting an assertive throttle-to-brake inversion command exceeding $90\%$ braking magnitude ($\text{brake}_t \ge 0.90$).
    \item \textbf{Root Mean Square Lane Deviation ($d_{\text{RMS}}$, m):} Quadratic mean cross-track displacement separating vehicular center from the roadway centerline over evaluation rollouts:
    \begin{equation}
        d_{\text{RMS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N d_{\text{center}, t}^2}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Maximum Excursion Boundary ($d_{\max}$, m):} Peak lateral displacement recorded during evaluation, quantifying proximity to the critical $3.0\text{ m}$ lane departure threshold.
    \item \textbf{Unsafe Failure Rate ($SF\%$, \%):} The statistical percentage of rollouts resulting in physical collisions, off-road lane departures ($>3.0\text{ m}$), or vehicular engine stalls exceeding $10\text{ seconds}$.
    \item \textbf{Relative Reward Sensitivity ($\Delta R_\%$, \%):} The normalized percentage performance drop under adversarial observation attack:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta R_\% = \frac{R_{\text{clean}} - R_{\text{atk}}}{R_{\text{clean}}} \times 100\%
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Model Inference Latency ($T_{\text{inf}}$, ms):} The forward pass compute duration isolated strictly to the actor network running on the embedded edge CPU.
    \item \textbf{Communication Transport Overhead ($T_{\text{rpc}}$, ms):} Serialized network round-trip delay to transmit observations and return action vectors across Ethernet TCP sockets.
    \item \textbf{Execution Jitter Variance ($\sigma^2_{\text{jitter}}$, $\text{ms}^2$):} Variance of loop turnaround times across continuous trajectories, quantifying temporal determinism.
    \item \textbf{Feature Attribution Score ($S_i$):} The expected gradient magnitude of policy action outputs with respect to latent coordinate $z_i \in \mathbb{R}^{95}$:
    \begin{equation}
        S_i = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{s} \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \left| \frac{\partial \pi_{\text{steer}}(\mathbf{s})}{\partial z_i} \right| + \left| \frac{\partial \pi_{\text{long}}(\mathbf{s})}{\partial z_i} \right| \right]
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup \& Edge Architecture}

\subsection{CARLA Simulation Platform \& Vehicle Dynamics}
All experiments execute in CARLA 0.9.15 on Windows 10 with an NVIDIA Quadro P6000 GPU (24GB VRAM) and Intel Xeon CPU. Server and client run synchronously at $20\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 0.05\text{ s}$), with PhysX rigid-body substepping at $100\text{ Hz}$ ($\delta t = 0.01\text{ s}$) to guarantee tire contact dynamics. The vehicle asset is a four-wheeled passenger vehicle (curb mass $1500\text{ kg}$, wheelbase $2.87\text{ m}$, tire friction $\mu = 1.0$). Benchmarks are conducted on Town01 (training and long straightaway navigation) and Town02 (zero-shot transfer across sharp intersections).

\subsection{Sensor Ladder Configurations}
We evaluate four systematic sensory rigs:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rung 1 (Monocular 100-d):} Single front semantic camera ($0^\circ, 125^\circ\text{ FOV}$), outputting 95-d VAE latent concatenated with 5-d telemetry $\mathbf{x}_{\text{nav}} = [v_t, d_{\text{center}}, \theta_{\text{heading}}, \delta_{t-1}, a_{\text{long}, t-1}]$.
    \item \textbf{Rung 2 (3-Cam Forward 290-d):} Front ($0^\circ, 125^\circ\text{ FOV}$), Left ($-60^\circ, 90^\circ\text{ FOV}$), and Right ($+60^\circ, 90^\circ\text{ FOV}$) cameras ($210^\circ$ panoramic forward arc).
    \item \textbf{Rung 3 (4-Cam Surround 385-d):} Front ($0^\circ$), Left ($-90^\circ$), Right ($+90^\circ$), and Rear ($180^\circ$) cameras ($360^\circ$ horizontal coverage).
    \item \textbf{Rung 4 (Cam+LiDAR BEV 195-d):} Front camera paired with a 32-channel 3D LiDAR projected into a 2D Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) height grid ($80 \times 160$ channels).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Numerical Stabilization of Sparse BEV Autoencoders}
Autoencoding sparse 2D BEV point clouds suffers from severe numerical instabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Float32 Exponential Overflow ($\text{NaN}$ loss):} Extreme negative log-variances produced by dense layers when fitting empty ground cells cause $\exp(\log \sigma^2) \to 0$ or float32 overflows in the KL divergence term $\mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}} = -\frac{1}{2}\sum (1 + \log \sigma^2 - \mu^2 - \sigma^2)$. We enforce hard bounding $\log \sigma \in [-15.0, 1.0]$, restoring stable training.
    \item \textbf{Latent Variance Explosion ($1.8\times 10^6$):} Reparameterization noise during inference without target normalization induces massive latent swings. Enforcing deterministic forward evaluation during execution bounds latent coordinates within stable operating regimes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Physical Embedded Edge Platform (PIL Architecture)}
The physical Processor-in-the-Loop testbed connects the CARLA GPU server to a standalone Raspberry Pi 4 Model B (Broadcom BCM2711 quad-core ARM Cortex-A72 CPU @ $1.5\text{ GHz}$, $4\text{ GB}$ LPDDR4 RAM, headless 64-bit Linux) over a dedicated Gigabit Ethernet TCP socket bridge on port 5000. At every simulation step, observations are transmitted to the edge node, which executes forward policy inference and returns actuation vectors to the simulator before deadline expiry.

\subsection{Post-Training Quantization}
To evaluate hardware acceleration on ARM Cortex-A72, models are profiled across three numerical precisions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TF-32 (Float32):} Unquantized server baseline executing on host workstation GPU.
    \item \textbf{Quantized FP16:} 16-bit half-precision floating-point edge execution via TFLite runtime.
    \item \textbf{Quantized INT8:} Fully quantized 8-bit integer weights and activations calibrated with representative dataset tensors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion \& Architectural Insights}

\subsection{The S-PPO Real-Time Dilemma}
Our cyber-physical profiling exposes a fundamental contradiction between theoretical robustness and embedded real-time safety. In theoretical literature, randomized smoothing is regarded as a gold standard because it provides certified radius guarantees. However, in physical embedded control, each decision cycle is subject to hard automotive deadlines ($\le 50\text{ ms}$). Serializing $M=8$ forward passes on an ARM Cortex-A72 inflates latency to $245.30\text{ ms}$, dropping control rate to $3.1\text{ Hz}$ and delaying braking response to $322.60\text{ ms}$. In a vehicle cruising at $30\text{ km/h}$ ($8.33\text{ m/s}$), this $204\text{ ms}$ delay adds $1.7\text{ meters}$ of uncontrolled forward travel before braking begins---a gap that frequently turns a near-miss into a fatal crash ($30\%$ failure rate in Table~\ref{tab:securedrive_benchmarks}). Robustness metrics that ignore cyber-physical execution latency are fundamentally insufficient for autonomous driving.

\subsection{Kinematic Cornering Limits \& Curvature Deceleration}
Closed-loop rollouts across all six formulations revealed an intrinsic physical boundary at sharp $90^\circ$ street corners ($R \approx 8\text{ m}$). Entering such corners at nominal cruising velocity ($v \approx 22\text{ km/h} \approx 6.1\text{ m/s}$) demands lateral acceleration of $a_y = v^2/R \approx 4.65\text{ m/s}^2$ ($\sim 0.47g$), approaching tire adhesion limits in CARLA and inducing severe front-wheel understeer. Concurrently, steering actuator low-pass filtering ($\tau \approx 0.33\text{ s}$) introduces an actuation delay corresponding to $8\text{--}9\text{ meters}$ of forward travel. This demonstrates that reactive state-space defenses must be coupled with predictive curvature-aware deceleration ($v_{\text{target}}(\kappa) = \max(10.0, 22.0 - 0.35|\theta_{\text{heading}}|)$) to reliably navigate tight urban intersections.

\subsection{Limitations of Flat Latent Concatenation \& Next Pathways}
While flat VAE latent concatenation enables fast linear policy heads, it destroys 2D spatial inductive bias and forces the downstream MLP to learn cross-modal alignments purely from scalar reward feedback. These findings motivate patch-based tokenization with multi-head cross-attention~\cite{transfuser2021, interfuser2022} and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models, which preserve metric spatial coordinates while enabling high-level semantic reasoning.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented \textbf{SecureDrive}, a comprehensive cyber-physical evaluation framework for robust deep reinforcement learning in autonomous driving. By evaluating an empirical sensor ladder alongside six robust PPO formulations on physical ARM Cortex-A72 edge hardware, we exposed the critical trade-offs between observation representation scaling, adversarial perturbation tolerance, and physical real-time execution deadlines. While inference-smoothed policies achieve certified robustness at the cost of catastrophic braking latency, our proposed INT8 ASR-PPO co-design achieves $0.0\%$ unsafe failure rate, $1134.10$ attacked return, and deterministic $8.4\text{ Hz}$ edge turnaround, demonstrating that effective automotive robustness requires holistic co-design across algorithms, actuators, and embedded hardware.
